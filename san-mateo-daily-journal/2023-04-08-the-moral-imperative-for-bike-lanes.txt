 The moral imperative for bike lanes 

Editor,

What parents need now more than ever is the ability to set their kids free and have them be safe.

Bike lanes in San Mateo are a solution. They make life easier, healthier and more fun for our community’s parents, kids and seniors. While it builds our kids’ resilience and independence, it also saves lives as well (https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2019/05/190529113036.htm).

Walking and biking myself around San Mateo, I see loads of bikes parked in the alleys of restaurants. Our community’s wageworkers cannot afford the cost of being a victim of traffic violence, and we cannot handle more cars on our roads.

Bike lanes also save families a jaw-dropping amount of money. The annual cost of car ownership now exceeds $10,000 per year (https://newsroom.aaa.com/2022/08/annual-cost-of-new-car-ownership-crosses-10k-mark/). Enabling households to drop one of their cars is a massive windfall for our community–and those savings go directly to helping residents avoid displacement and stay in their homes.

The benefits of bike lanes are both quantifiable and unquantifiable. They are quantifiable as more households build wealth by opting for fewer cars — reducing traffic and competitive street parking.

And they are unquantifiable: a young generation of San Mateans who are independent, resilient and responsible — prepared to participate in our community fully without needing to be 16 years old or having $10,000.

Max Mautner

San Mateo
